# 4. Existing systems

### DIAGNOSIS · ALTERNATIVES · INTERVENTION PLAN

# Regain decision-making capacity over a system your business depends on.

Operations are degrading and it's hard to assess the vendor's explanations or proposals. My job is to turn symptoms into causes, alternatives and priorities.

The signs usually appear gradually:

* every feature takes longer than expected;
* changing one part breaks another;
* some components nobody wants to touch;
* deployments create uncertainty;
* performance or scalability problems appear;
* integrations are increasingly hard to maintain;
* technical debt constrains product decisions;
* a migration seems inevitable, but nobody is clear on where to start;
* it's unclear whether to fix the system, change how you work with the vendor, or replace components.

My work doesn't start by assuming you need a rewrite, or that I should carry out the changes.

It starts by understanding **what is really limiting the system, how it is maintained, and what consequence that has for the business.**

### Assess

Review the architecture, maintenance, operations, integrations, the vendor's proposals, and the relevant risks and dependencies.

### Compare

Compare what to fix, keep, modernize progressively, replace in parts, improve in how you work with the vendor, or resolve by changing vendors.

Make costs, benefits, risks and trade-offs explicit, including the option of not intervening yet.

### Recommend

Deliver a well-supported recommendation and a plan ordered by priority, cost, dependencies and sequence.

### Support

The diagnosis and the plan must be useful on their own, even if another vendor carries out the changes. If you want me to support or technically oversee the execution, that scope is agreed separately.

## The outcome we aim for

**Regain the ability to decide how to change the software, with a reasonable balance between speed, cost and risk.**

The diagnosis is contracted at a fixed price based on the agreed scope.

# 5. New software-based ventures

### PRODUCT · TECHNOLOGY · BUSINESS

# Decide which uncertainty to resolve before committing to a larger investment.

I work with companies that already operate, have customers or know their industry and want to open a new software-based line of business.

The first decision isn't always what to build. It may be what to validate about demand, willingness to pay, technical feasibility, integration or operating cost.

### Identify critical hypotheses

Define what we need to know and what evidence would allow us to make a decision.

The existence of a market requires commercial evidence; technical analysis does not replace it.

### Design the right test

Choose the experiment that resolves the priority uncertainty. Sometimes a manual test will be enough; other times we'll need to build software.

### Scope the prototype, if needed

Define the minimum scope, budget and dependencies of a technical test without turning it into a full product too early.

### Decide in stages

Set criteria to continue, change course or stop the investment, and agree on what evidence unlocks the next stage.

The validation plan is contracted in stages. The budget and decision criteria are agreed before each one.

## The outcome we aim for

**Reduce uncertainty before increasing investment.**

# 6. Fractional software architect

### TECHNICAL LEADERSHIP · ARCHITECTURE · DECISION

# I take on a defined technical responsibility within a project.

Fractional architecture describes the engagement model, not the scope. The company and I agree on which decisions and functions it delegates, and what authority, dedication, availability and limits my involvement will have.

The scope may include:

* initial architecture and review of critical decisions;
* standards and team support;
* technical coordination with the end client;
* alignment with whoever approves the budget and with those who will enforce the decisions.

Before starting, we confirm that the project has sufficient development capacity. My involvement covers a leadership gap; **it does not replace the people needed to execute.**

The engagement is agreed as a monthly fee tied to the defined dedication and responsibility.

# 7. Principles

### HOW I THINK ABOUT SOFTWARE

# The same judgment; the decision changes.

There is no perfect architecture, and no technology that solves a business problem on its own.

I understand the context, compare alternatives and recommend based on evidence. Then I support an execution that can be observed and measured.

## Context

What the business needs to achieve and what capacity it has to do so.

## Evidence

What we know and which uncertainty is best resolved first.

## Alternatives

Which options are viable and what costs, risks and dependencies they entail.

## Trade-offs

What we gain, what we sacrifice and what we decide to postpone.

Every recommendation must explain what it solves, what it demands and what consequences it introduces.
